Buyers keep their offer. What changes is who they top up.

When a BOSS Revolution user tops up the same person again, 71% of the time it is the exact same offer and 92% of the time the same product type. Most offer changes between one purchase and the next happen because the next top-up is for someone else, often on another carrier.

The answer: repeat for the same person, change for a different one

For each buyer, we took their first successful top-up after 30 June and looked at the very next one. 56% bought again within 30 days. The next purchase went to the same recipient only 30% of the time, and those repeats are highly loyal. Next purchases for a different recipient still reuse the same offer 34% of the time, and 52% of the time when the new recipient is on the same carrier.

For the same person, the offer rarely changes

The share of same-recipient repeats that keep the offer ID stays between 63% and 76% across tenure segments and every robustness check. It is lowest for new buyers, who are still finding their offer, and highest for established buyers and for regular (non-subscription) purchases.

When the offer does change for the same person, it is mostly a different denomination within the same product type. Catalog churn and carrier changes are negligible: only 544 switches kept the same carrier and amount under a new offer ID, and 32 followed a change of the recipient's carrier (same recipient, carrier and amount, same recipient and carrier, same recipient and type).

Quick-send makes repeating automatic, but browsers repeat too

The entry point of the repeat purchase (top_up_started_from, from DCS-4011) shows how much of the loyalty is the interface and how much is choice. One-tap repeats pre-fill the last order, so they are close to 100% by design. The browse flows are the cleaner test of preference: the buyer sees the offer list and most still pick the offer they bought last time.

Where buyers switch more

Data packs and a few markets switch more than average. These are the places where the offer list, promotions and featured offers have the most room to change what people buy.

Over a whole quarter, the same pattern

Across 506,743 buyers and 2,070,629 purchases from 1 July to 27 September, the average buyer uses fewer distinct offers than they have recipients, whatever their frequency. Among buyers with exactly two purchases, 48% bought the same offer twice, even though 59% of them topped up two different people.

What this means for the product

Put the last offer first in the browse flows

In the recents list, people page and search, 54% of same-recipient repeats end on the same offer after scrolling the full list. Showing "last sent to this person" at the top of the offer list would shorten the path for most of them. Check what the list pre-selects today before building.

Subscriptions automate a habit that already exists

50,627 buyers in this cohort repeated the exact same offer to the same person within 30 days. They are the natural audience for a subscription offer on that recipient, and the offer to propose is the one they just bought.

Upselling needs a nudge

Denomination changes split 47% up and 53% down, so amounts do not rise on their own. The 29% of same-recipient repeats that already change offer, and the data-pack and Haiti, Jamaica and Nigeria buyers, are the most open to featured offers and promotions.

Senders have a go-to offer

24% of all next purchases send the same offer to a different person on the same carrier. Promotions set at offer level therefore reach more than one recipient per sender.

Close the measurement gaps

Offer-list position and a "pre-selected" flag on the order would separate accepted defaults from active choices; neither is tracked today (see the IMTU Amplitude events audit). Subscription renewals are server-side and never reach Amplitude, so the true repeat rate of the same offer is higher than shown here.

Method and caveats

Pairing each purchase with the next one
Amplitude funnels with the Nth-time filter on MTUOrderStatusSuccessScr: step 1 is a buyer's 1st purchase counted from 30 June (1-day lookback), step 2 their 2nd, within 30 days. The anchoring was validated: the "2nd purchase" step returned 148,089 users against 145,580 users with 2+ purchases in the same window. Each buyer contributes one pair, so heavy buyers are not over-weighted.
What "same" means
Each chart holds one or more properties constant across the two steps: recipient_phone_number, offer_id, recipient_carrier, offer_amount, offer_type. All are populated on 100% of successful orders in the last 30 days. Recipient numbers are consistently E.164 (99.96% carry a "+" prefix), so one person is not split across formats. Offer IDs are carrier SKUs such as TIGO_GT-US-PAQUETIGO-10 or CLARO_DO_6; amounts are in the sender's currency (98% USD).
Segments
Established = 3+ purchases from 1 April to 29 June 2026 (201,967 buyers), light = 1-2 (111,171), new or lapsed = none (108,904).
Not covered
The Money app (project 420385), subscription renewals (server-side, not in Amplitude) and anything before July 2026. Subscription creations are 25% of successful orders in the last 30 days; excluding them raises same-recipient loyalty from 71.0% to 75.6%.
