These are the ones where two or more people took different positions, or where someone asked for a change and nobody closed it out. Nothing here was settled.

Shmuel, Emilio, Zev 55:42 to 1:01:58

AskNarrow the "toggle off when any subscription is active" rule

This took the longest stretch of the session and nobody agreed with the rule as you described it. The objection is to the scope, not the intent.

  • Emilio wants it scoped to the same corridor (same country, carrier and recipient), calling a global off "premature". His evidence: 90% of senders do one to three top ups a month, and the recipient map is roughly 50% to 60% sending to one recipient, plus about 20% to two and 15% to three. He specifically wants the same recipient, same carrier, different denomination case protected, and offered to fine tune it offline.
  • Shmuel does not accept a blanket off at all: if someone is sending to a new recipient, a grandfather in his example, there is no reason to default them off.
  • Zev added the switch case: a user who cancels only to move the same recipient to a cheaper denomination should keep the toggle on, because that cancel was a plan change, not a rejection. But a user who just toggled off and immediately re enters to buy the same plan should stay unchecked.
Where it landed Emilio owns fine tuning it with you offline. Your own counter, that a blanket off pulls roughly 40% of the base out of the offer entirely, was accepted as a reason to test rather than ship it.
Vitaly vs Emilio 1:02:01 and 1:08:30

OpenPredictable default, or one that anticipates behaviour

Vitaly: nobody knows the rules, so a default that changes shape from trip to trip may just confuse people. They unsubscribe and it is off, they try again and it is off, they stop checking, and then it is on. He argued predictability, even always on, may be the better product, and repeated it after seeing the new bottom sheet: with the subscription that visible, always on stops being confusing.

Emilio: good interface design is about eliminating friction and anticipating obvious behaviour, so making the call for the user is the job. He accepts the inconsistency because it is confined to one narrow use case.

Where it landed Genuinely split, and the cheapest of these to settle with an A/B rather than an argument.
Emilio, with Shmuel 1:17:44

AskHold the subscription promo back for the cancellation flow

Emilio flagged two tickets as critical: the cancellation flow, which he called essential to developing subscriptions and retention, and the subscription promo, where backend and frontend work is already done.

His position on the promo is a direct challenge to how you presented it. If someone is already paying full price and subscribing, discounting them immediately gives away margin for nothing. Fire the offer at cancellation instead: hold the discount until they try to leave, then save them. He pointed at the Whoop cancellation flow as the model and Shmuel agreed, joking about putting users through "the same 9 step program".

Tension to resolve You presented promos as a first payment discount plus a recurring one. Emilio wants save offer only, at least to start. Decide whether both can run, or whether acquisition promo waits.
Jonathan vs Shmuel 1:11:31

OpenA recurring savings header on the home screen

Jonathan asked for a header that subscribers see regularly when they open the app, reinforcing that they have a subscription and that it is saving them money. He expects that header image to perform well.

Shmuel said plainly he does not think it is a good idea, and gave no reason when asked.

Vitaly offered the compromise that is on the table: a company he uses in Poland shows him the saved amount at the moment of each purchase, not permanently. Same reinforcement, tied to a transaction instead of the home screen.

Where it landed Unresolved between two of the most senior voices in the room. Vitaly's purchase moment placement is the version most likely to clear both.
Shmuel 56:30

AskIf the toggle defaults off, ask for the opt in explicitly

Shmuel's condition on accepting any off default: do not leave it as a silent toggle the user has to find. Put the question to them, along the lines of "do you want to make this every 30 days", and attach the offer to that same moment so the ask and the incentive arrive together.

Challenges to the numbers

4 items · fix before the deck circulates

None of these dispute the results. They dispute how three of the slides can be read, which matters more now that Jonathan and Esty want to reuse the format.

Emilio49:37

ChallengeThe toggle off denominator is shrinking

The population you show the toggle to keeps getting smaller, because once someone is on a subscription they only see the toggle again if they come back to top up manually. So the rising toggle off rate is partly a composition effect, not purely users learning what the toggle does. Re cut that trend on a stable denominator before repeating the "users are learning" reading.

Emilio45:34

Challenge16% is a share, not a volume

Sixteen percent of bad reviews is a big share, but ten complaints set against the month's new subscription volume is very little. Keep both framings on the slide so the percentage does not read as a crisis on its own.

Zev46:14

ChallengeYou are measuring reviews, not ratings

On the App Store a review is when someone writes something and a rating is when they only leave stars. The Slack bot only catches the written ones, so the slide is a reviews trend. Label it that way, and consider whether the star only ratings are moving differently.

Emilio, Esty48:11 and 52:02

ChallengeTwo framing fixes on the behaviour slides

  • Emilio: state on the slide that monthly is the dominant subscription frequency. Without it, the cancellation spike on day 30 looks unexplained rather than expected.
  • Esty: "the good news is that 50% or so left it on." You currently lead with 46% turning it off. The same number reads very differently the other way round.
