Moving eGift decisioning off IDT Pay

What DCS has to do to hand eGift fraud decisioning to an external vendor, what it must settle before signing anything, and why the six-week integration estimate is not the number that sets the date.

Prepared for Emilio DelRio, VP Boss Revolution Date 15 September 2026 Scope DTC eGift, Boss Revolution app Sprint DCS 77 (9-22 Sep)

Where this starts

read this first

The vendor review is real and the channel is genuinely blocked. But the brief that came out of the meeting contains three factual errors, and each one changes what DCS should build, what it should pay, and when it can be live. They are corrected below before the plan, because the plan only makes sense afterwards.

Everything in this document was checked against Jira, Confluence and Amplitude on 15 September 2026. Where a claim could not be verified it says so. The three headline figures were re-run directly against Amplitude project 650506 rather than taken from the meeting notes.

Correction 1 · the target

Brief: "~90% of eGift transactions are failing due to strict IDT Pay fraud rules."

There is no IDT Pay rules table to switch off, and IDT Pay is only one of at least five gates

IDT Pay's fraud decisioning is Accertify, a third-party vendor already contracted, adapter-built and PCI-integrated, called from inside IDTPay. The documented chain is mtu-int-api → payment-int-api → IDTPay → Accertify, per IMTU Wallet: Accertify Fraud Integration.

Four further gates decline an eGift purchase independently: Fraudzilla velocity rules, which DCS/DTC owns and IDT Pay does not (DTC Fraudzilla); Jube, which surfaces as "Error 59"; K2's own velocity and location screens (VelocityMatchFail*, LocationScreenMatch*, AniLocked); and ShieldWall on the Zendit rails, which fails closed on timeout (ShieldWall 2.0).

This is not academic. BGW-9535 is a live production defect titled "Transaction which was accepted by Sardine, still failed at antifraud". A vendor approval does not produce an approved transaction while a second gate still runs. Standing IDT Pay down for eGift is a functional requirement of the project, not a courtesy.

Correction 2 · the money

Brief: "quoted approval rates of 88-90% and fees of roughly 1-1.5% per transaction."

41% of eGift failures are issuer declines that no fraud vendor can convert, and the fee is charged on all approved GMV

Over the clean window 1 March to 31 July 2026, 41.2% of eGift failures carry an issuer-side card decline: insufficient funds, expired, invalid or restricted card. Those decline identically under nSure or Riskified. Only 1.9% carry a velocity or limit code. The remaining 55.3% is the opaque literal failed, which is where the addressable pool lives and which nobody has split. See the chart below.

Both vendors bill on total approved GMV, not on incremental approvals. Gift cards run a roughly 3 to 8% merchant commission, so a 1% fee consumes between one eighth and one third of gross margin on every approved transaction, including the ones IDT was already approving. At the low end of that band the tier choice between 1% and 1.5% is material, not a rounding difference. Finance needs a sensitivity table over take rate and approval lift, not a point estimate.

Correction 3 · the baseline

Brief: implied the current decline rate is the number to price against.

IDT switched Visa off on DTC eGift 18 days ago and never reverted it

EGIFT-858 "[Potential Fraud] DTC Visa", description "Disable Visa in DTC", was created and closed within 61 seconds on 28 August 2026. There is no revert ticket, no review date and no owner. It inflates the current decline rate by about 7 percentage points: 89.1% in the fortnight before, 96.1% in the fortnight after.

Separately, 85% of August's successes fall inside 25-31 August, the documented account-farm window (BRS-56097), where the decline rate fell to its yearly low because fraud was being approved. If that month goes into Riskified's six-month pricing pack unflagged, IDT hands over a window whose approvals are disproportionately fraudulent and gets a worse rate quoted back.

Two tracks

the single most important structural choice

Put the decision server-side and the app store never sits on the critical path. Put it in an SDK and the schedule is hostage to a release train that already closed for September.

Track A · the pilot path

Server-side decision call in payment-int-api

Call the vendor from hdm-go-payment-int-api, scoped to eGift by product type. This has a shipped reference implementation: DCS-3949 put Accertify behind wallet transactions from exactly this service.

  • No app release, so no store review and no train dependency
  • The kill switch is our own config and reverts instantly
  • The request payload is already assembled at the right point: product_type, channel, app_group, offer_id, time_on_file, origin_ip, user_agent
  • Per-product carve-out already shipped and verified in DCS-3472, so IMTU and eSIM need not be touched

Track B · parallel and conditional

Vendor mobile SDK and the decline surface

Only becomes the critical path if the vendor cannot decision acceptably without device telemetry. If it does, the whole plan re-dates by roughly 10 to 14 weeks. Get that answer in writing before the decision.

  • The BR app is a single Flutter monorepo. Neither vendor was stated to ship a Flutter plugin
  • eGift has no feature flags at all today, so a client kill switch is net-new code in a shipped binary
  • ATF-2666 records that a native fraud SDK starts before Dart on Android and at plugin registration on iOS, so a remote flag cannot stop it in the launch where you need it
  • Store privacy review is the gate that slips: it is AT RISK and unassigned right now on the live AppsFlyer migration

  • Decide whether a step-up (CVV or 3DS) path is offered on a soft decline. That also settles the CVV question Erik Bole raised on ATF-2527 that nobody answered
  • Build a review or challenge queue only if a vendor actually returns a review verdict, and only with a named queue owner and an SLA
Exit criteria
  • A written answer exists on whether the vendor requires SDK telemetry to decision acceptably. If yes, this moves onto the critical path and the whole plan re-dates
  • The SDK-versus-risk_signals overlap is resolved: replace, wrap, or run alongside
  • The kill-switch flag compiles with legacy decisioning as its default, verified by test with remote config unavailable
  • iOS App Privacy and Play Data Safety declarations updated, with a named owner per platform
  • Physical-device QA pass on both apps. Emulator crashes in vendor security SDKs already forced real-device testing once
  • Decline copy and a retry path are live, and the pilot measures completed purchases rather than approval rate alone

Risks worth the room's attention

ranked by how likely they are to actually happen

The vendor is bought to fix declines it cannot move

Impact

41.2% of failures are issuer declines that decline identically under any vendor, and velocity is 1.9%. Both vendors bill on total approved GMV regardless. At a 3% take rate a 1% fee eats a third of gross margin on every approved transaction, including the ones IDT was already approving.

Mitigation

Do not sign a percentage-of-approved-GMV contract until the opaque bucket is split by IDTPay response code. State the addressable pool as a bounded range in the decision paper. Price the fee against the whole approved base, never against incremental approvals.

Owner: DCS TPM for the decomposition, VP Boss Revolution for the commercial consequence

Vendor approval does not produce an approved transaction

Impact

eGift declines at up to five independent gates. A vendor replaces at most one or two. The channel stays blocked after go-live while IDT pays on approved GMV, and the vendor is billed for transactions K2 subsequently rejects. BGW-9535 is the live proof of exactly this failure mode with Sardine.

Mitigation

Make the IDT Pay stand-down a functional exit criterion of Phase 3, verified in a non-production environment before the pilot. Inventory K2-sourced declines first. Define "approved" contractually, and build an independent count that does not come from vendor reporting.

Owner: DCS Backend with IDT Pay

The pilot reads out on a metric that structurally cannot have gone wrong yet

Impact

A four-week pilot declares victory on approval rate having seen almost none of the fraud it let through, and the room approves full cutover on evidence that cannot contradict it. ATF-2801 documents the downside already realised at IDT: a product running with no effective fraud rules, silently passing everything, roughly $70K.

Mitigation

Minimum eight weeks of live advisory traffic. A pending-label state on every record from day one. Read-out date separated from traffic-stop date in the charter. Duration, thresholds and approvers agreed before the pilot starts, with unexplained differences blocking sign-off.

Owner: DCS TPM for the charter, approvers named at the PDR

The liability transfer does not cover the fraud that caused the clampdown

Impact

The documented eGift vector is account takeover on the genuine cardholder's own saved card. That commonly falls outside standard fraud-liability definitions and into service-dispute or friendly-fraud categories. IDT pays a liability fee that does not indemnify the loss it was bought to cover, and finds out on the first real ATO chargeback after cutover.

Mitigation

Put the exact DCS-3472 scenario to both vendors in writing before signature and get the liability category confirmed. It may be the whole difference between nSure's 1% and 1.5% tiers, and it may be excluded by both. If excluded, the business case is approval-rate recovery only and must be re-argued on that basis.

Owner: VP Boss Revolution with Legal

Buy-versus-build is never resolved and the decision is reopened at architecture review

Impact

IDT signs a chargeback-liability contract that cuts across a live FY2026 board commitment, selected by a process CAF never saw. The defensible DCS precedent is DCS-4461, where a question put to IDT Pay and the fraud team went unanswered for four and a half months and the ticket closed with it still open.

Mitigation

Route the decision as a PDR, which compels IDT Pay and CAF attendance. Put the single outstanding ask to Mark Moorman in writing with a date. State plainly that the Terminus PDR itself carries no accepted status and a TBD section, so it is not a settled architecture either, and that eGift has zero delivery tickets in ATF.

Owner: VP Boss Revolution to convene, DCS TPM to draft

The kill switch cannot ship when it is needed

Impact

eGift has no feature flags at all today, so a client-side kill switch is net-new code in a shipped binary. A native SDK starts before Dart on Android and at plugin registration on iOS, so a remote flag cannot stop it in the current launch. Week 5 is then spent discovering the rollback lever cannot ship until the next train.

Mitigation

Keep the decision server-side, where the call site is ours and disables instantly. If the vendor requires SDK telemetry, say so at the PDR and re-date the plan there rather than in December. Compile any client flag with legacy decisioning as its default, absent or invalid reading as OFF.

Owner: Yuri Rykovsky for the server-side call, DCS App lead for the flag

A scoping mistake degrades IMTU or eSIM

Impact

All three products share one payment path, one Store API metadata contract and one admin surface. The eGift rule change is made on the same screen that governs IMTU velocity rules. Revenue loss on two products that work, caused by a change intended for one that does not.

Mitigation

Per-product config already exists and shipped, so the carve-out is config rather than new code. Add four-eyes review on rule changes, a change window, a captured pre and post diff of IMTU rule state, and use the existing config-change notification email as the audit trail. IMTU and eSIM regression is a Phase 3 exit criterion.

Owner: DCS Backend with CAF

Approval-rate gains do not convert into completed purchases

Impact

The customer sees generic decline text with no reason and no retry path. The pilot reports a lift that never shows up in revenue, and the business case is judged on the wrong metric.

Mitigation

Put decline copy and a retry path in Track B scope with a named design owner, driven by the Phase 0 decomposition so only reasons worth surfacing are surfaced. Measure completed purchases in the charter, not approval rate alone.

Owner: DCS Design and DCS App

The client workstream lands unbudgeted on a bench of three

Impact

The whole DCS app bench is three Flutter developers already carrying IMTU subscriptions. The house comparable is stark: ATF-2666 has run 67 days and is still not enforcing in production, and ATF-2727, explicitly not the production rollout, took 26 days on its own.

Mitigation

Get the Flutter answer in writing before the decision and keep Track B off the critical path. Require a measured bundle, cold-start and CPU delta from a real build before signature. Copy the ATF-2727 scope list as the estimate template.

Owner: DCS App lead with Yuri Rykovsky

Capacity collision in December

Impact

A schedule whose clock starts at a November signature runs its integration and pilot ramp straight into the lowest-capacity fortnight of the year. On the verified 14-day cadence, Sprint 84 projects to roughly 16-29 December.

Mitigation

Discount that sprint's capacity explicitly. Aim for code-complete by 1 December so the December window carries advisory-run monitoring rather than build. Look up real sprint ids once the board is extended: ids are non-sequential and cannot be inferred.
